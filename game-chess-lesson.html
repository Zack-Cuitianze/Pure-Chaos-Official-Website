Pure Chaos // Chess Lesson

How chess works.

Six short sections, no jargon. Read it once, then play the beginner level — the board will show you every legal move.

01 The board

Sixty-four squares, eight by eight, taking turns light and dark. White always moves first, then players alternate — one move each.

Columns are called files (a–h, left to right). Rows are ranks (1–8, from White's side). The board starts with White's pieces on ranks 1–2 and Black's on ranks 7–8.

02 The pieces

Each piece moves in its own pattern. Dots show every square the piece could move to on an empty board.

03 Capturing

You take an enemy piece by landing on its square. The knight below can jump to d5 and take the black pawn — the ring marks a capture.

You can never land on your own pieces, and you never jump over anything except with the knight.

04 Check and checkmate

When your king is attacked, that is check — you must answer it immediately: move the king, block the attack, or take the attacker.

If the king is attacked and there is no legal answer, that is checkmate: the game is over. The second board shows a classic back-rank mate.

05 The three special moves

These catch out almost every new player. All three are always available — the game handles them for you.

Click any square the knight can move to.

06 How a game ends

  • Checkmate — the king cannot escape. You win.
  • Time out — in a timed game, run out of clock and you lose.
  • Stalemate — no legal move, but not in check. Draw.
  • Draw — not enough material, fifty quiet moves, or the same position three times.

That is everything you need. The beginner level plays slowly and the board highlights your options.
